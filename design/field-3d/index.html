Volumetric languages for one molecular field

Every tile raymarches the same real volume: aspirin's electrostatic potential from an RHF/STO-3G calculation in PySCF, with the self-consistent electron density on a second channel so any language that wants a molecular surface has one. One WebGL2 context, one uploaded texture, one shared camera — drag any tile and all of them turn together.

The flat study asked what mark to use. Three dimensions asks a harder question, because here every language must also decide what to occlude. A slice shows one plane and hides sixty-three. An isosurface shows one level and hides both sides of it. A volume integral shows everything and locates nothing. There is no honest 3D display of a scalar field that throws nothing away — so the comparison is not which is prettiest, but which loss a particular reader can afford.

Those three are one instrument, not three candidates

Silhouette, gradient magnitude and volumetric LIC are the same discovery three times. Every one is dark-ground, additive and non-occluding: the field arrives as light rather than as plastic, and the molecule stays visible through it. That is a compositing model, not a look — and additive light is commutative, so they do not fight when stacked. The neutral shell (33) is already in the same family.

They also share one gap, and in this domain it is the worst one available: none of them carries sign. |∇V| discards it by construction, LIC's noise destroys it, a rim is the thinnest possible carrier for a hue. Donor versus acceptor is the first question anyone asks of an electrostatic potential. Three languages that cannot answer it are an atmosphere, not an instrument.

So: treat them as separable channels of one render and add back the one they are all missing. Switch each off to see what it was carrying.

The specimen is a real calculation, and its numbers are stated because they bound every picture above. The potential is clamped to ±35 kcal/mol — not a percentile, a chemistry decision: outside the 0.001 a.u. density surface, where the potential is the quantity anyone acts on, the field spans −36.5 to +34.9 kcal/mol, so the display uses its whole range there. Inside the surface it clips, and it should: the raw maximum is +66,808 kcal/mol at a carbon nucleus, and a ramp honest to that number would render every chemically meaningful value as the same shade of neutral.

The box is faded, and that is a display choice. The cube is where the calculation exists; past its faces nothing was computed. Stopping hard at the face draws that ignorance as a solid hexagonal solid, and every volume-integrating tile rendered the bounding box as though the box were part of the molecule. The outer 7% of each axis is therefore windowed to zero — stated here for the same reason the clamp is: it is the display, not the physics.

The volume is 8-bit. 64³ voxels, two channels, quantised at 0.275 kcal/mol per level and deflated to 390 KB so the page carries its own data. That is finer than the difference between any two of these renderings, but it is a real floor and it is stated rather than implied. The density channel is stored as log₁₀ρ over six decades, which puts the standard 0.001 a.u. surface at byte 85 — comfortably inside the resolved range rather than at the end of it.

Each tile reports its own measured cost in milliseconds, top right, as a rolling median at the current ray-step count. In the flat study "cost" was a rhetorical device. Here it is a number, it differs by more than an order of magnitude across the families, and a language that cannot hold a frame budget is not available to an interactive panel no matter how well it reads.
