Night candidates · 16 worlds

Day is a tech company; night is cyberpunk. Each card below is a different hue architecture, not a shift of the same one — where the accent sits, what opposes it, whether the ground is cold or warm, and how much chroma it spends. Every one is rendered on the real Fields panel markup at real sizes, because a colour is not a colour until it is next to 10 px secondary text and a 2 px border: swatches agree with each other far more than interfaces do.

The numbers are the repo's own bars, computed per candidate, not opinions: body text needs 4.5:1, non-text UI colour 3.0:1, and a diverging viz pair needs ΔE ≥ 0.10 or blue-positive and red-negative read as one colour on a screenshot. 16 of 16 pass all of them. Peak chroma is stated because it is the dial: the incumbent spends 0.088, the loudest here spends 0.20, and "how neon" is that one number.

One thing deliberately does NOT follow the theme. The field colours keep chemistry convention — blue positive, red negative — even where that clashes with the accent, which you can see most clearly in Nuclear Lime: a lime interface around a blue/red field. Theming those two would make a chemist ask which palette they were looking at before they could read a sign, and the whole point of a convention is that the answer never depends on that. The UI is the theme's; the data is the discipline's.
