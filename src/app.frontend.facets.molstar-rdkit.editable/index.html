σ-hole · surface electrostatics

A halogen with no σ-hole cannot make a halogen bond however good the geometry looks. The angle is the evidence: below 150° from the C–X axis a positive cap is not a σ-hole.

STO-3G is not offered here, although it is three times faster and the cost gate recommends it. Measured on this backend: it finds zero σ-holes on bromobenzene and chlorobenzene, both of which have one at def2-SVP (44.0 and 31.6 kcal/mol). A minimal basis does not shrink the answer, it erases it — every halogen comes back looking like fluorine. For a large ligand raise the budget instead; that costs time, not the conclusion.

electronics vs geometry

Depth alone does not make a halogen bond; the hole has to point at a Lewis base. Under each verdict, greyed, is what a purely geometric profiler would report — PLIP's own constants, and PLIP accepts fluorine as a halogen-bond donor on geometry alone. Where the two rows disagree is the point.

Torsional strain

A deposited or docked pose is fitted, not minimised, and routinely sits several kcal/mol above its own relaxed geometry — energy that comes straight out of the binding free energy.
